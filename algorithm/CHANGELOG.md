# The algorithm changelog

One entry per algorithm version, newest first. The id is `algo-<version>+<hash>`: the version is for people, the hash is the first 12 hex characters of the sha256 of `manifest.json`'s canonical JSON, and it is the proof. `manifest.json` is generated by `cargo run -p cli -- algo manifest --write` and checked by `backend/crates/algo/tests/manifest.rs`; `scripts/algorithm/check.mjs` fails a pull request whose components changed without the bump below and an entry here (ADRs 0104 and 0105).

- Major: layer 1 to 3 output can change (a protocol, a constant, a detector, the ranking, the trainer or the focus rules). The replay goldens in `backend/fixtures/replay/` change with it, and the Tier 1 scorecard is rerun.
- Minor: only the language layer changes (a prompt file, a model id, the validator). The goldens stay as they are.
- Patch: no output changes (a refactor or a comment in a digested file). The goldens must be byte-identical, which is what proves it.

Each entry says what changed, why, the ADR, and the benchmark delta (the scorecard before and after; `cli bench compare` once it exists).

## algo-6.0.0+f40a2b27677a

2026-11-27. The trajectory's rating verdict comes back, from a rule that counts the rating's memory and compares your first games with your last, as EXP-0030 decided.

- What changed: `profile::coaching::trajectory::RatingRule::PRODUCTION` is `MemoryHalves`. "Then" is the first `W = min(30, n / 2)` rated games and "now" the last `W`; no window is placed on the peak. The rating row's 80% interval comes from the player's own rating steps under a mean-reverting model (`Memory`: `a = min(q s / 2, 1)` with `q = ln 10 / 400`, `φ = 1 − a`, `σ² = s² / (a (2 − a))`, and the exact variance of the difference of two block means), not from the bootstrap of the window's ratings. `change` is the difference the verdict tests, the last window's mean minus the first window's. The trajectory names its rule (`ratingRule`, a new optional field of the contract, `memory-halves`). `coaching_input::trajectory::RATING_VERDICT_SHOWN` is `true`, from EXP-0030 (`RATING_VERDICT_SOURCE`), and `player_visible` passes only a trajectory from `CLEARED_RULE`, so every input built before this version still has its verdict withheld when the coach reads it; the manifest names the rule (`profile.trajectory_rating_verdict.rule`). The web's `computeTrajectory` is the same rule (`backend/fixtures/trajectory/cases.json`). The coach's prompts are `report/report.v6.md` and `chat/system.v8.md`: the change between the windows' averages, the peak as a plain fact with no difference against it, and a verdict that says the rating moved by more than its own wander, not why. The Progress headline and the Overview card say "Your rapid rating averaged 1,492 over your last 30 games, down from 1,560 over your first 30 (−68). It is 1,488 now. Your highest was 1,610, in April 2026."; the then | now table's rating row has its verdict and range again. `peak-bootstrap` stays selectable in the lab.
- Why: on 2,000 fresh simulated players whose strength never changed (`EXP-0030-fb2f150c7574`), `memory-halves` said "falling" to 0.103, 0.117 and 0.105 and "rising" to 0.089, 0.084 and 0.095 at 60, 120 and 250 rated games, every cell within the bound of 0.15 at 1 − 0.025/2 (the worst upper end 0.136), against the old rule's 0.46 to 0.79; it caught a 200-point fall 0.72, 0.84 and 0.99 of the time, and passed the rough-world (0.125 to 0.144), chess.com-like (0.086 to 0.107) and real-cohort guards (`lab/experiments/EXP-0030/decision.md`). The real Lichess series flagged 36% of settled players either way against the model's 20%, so the rate on real players is not yet known (EXP-0032), and long histories and new accounts are untested (EXP-0031).
- ADRs: 0117 (the rule and where it is stated), 0116 (the hide it lifts), 0105 (the version policy), 0109 (the arena).
- Benchmark: the replay goldens change only in their header (no replay fixture account has 20 rated games in a time class). The lab's trajectory golden response changes with the rule: `lab-peaked`, which climbs to a peak and falls back towards its start, is now `flat`, and `peak-bootstrap` still calls it `falling`. The arena's EXP-0015 record of this version is the new rule on EXP-0015's registered seeds.

## algo-5.0.1+d9c1f47013b6

2026-11-27. EXP-0030's two candidate rules for the trajectory's rating verdict, selectable in the lab only, with no output change.

- What changed: `profile::coaching::trajectory::RatingRule` names the rule of the rating row: `peak-bootstrap` (`RatingRule::PRODUCTION`, the rule of every version so far) and EXP-0030's frozen candidates `memory-halves` and `memory-before`, whose interval comes from the rating's own steps (`Memory`, `memory_compare`) instead of the bootstrap, against the first games or every game before the last ones, with no selected peak. `compute` runs the production rule and `compute_with` any of them; only `cli lab trajectory` selects another, through the request's `rule`. The worker and the web never do, so production computes exactly what it did. Only `profile.source_digest` moved.
- Why: EXP-0030 measures the candidates on the same requests as the champion (`lab/experiments/EXP-0030/preregistration.md`), after EXP-0015 hid the verdict (ADR 0116).
- ADRs: 0105 (the version policy), 0109 (the arena), 0116 (the hidden verdict).
- Benchmark: no change. `backend/crates/profile/tests/trajectory.rs` replays the web's fixture unchanged, the replay goldens and the lab's golden responses are byte-identical, and no component other than a source digest moved, so the Tier 1 scorecard of 5.0.0 stands.

## algo-5.0.0+3cabedf4c935

2026-11-26. The trajectory's rating verdict is computed and no longer stated to players or the coach, as EXP-0015 decided (#136).

- What changed: `coaching_input::trajectory::RATING_VERDICT_SHOWN` is `false`, from EXP-0015 (`RATING_VERDICT_SOURCE`); the manifest names it (`profile.trajectory_rating_verdict`). `coaching_input::trajectory::player_visible` cuts the CoachingInput's trajectory: `kind` is null, the rating row's verdict is the new `withheld` and its ranges (each window's and the difference's) are null, while its means, n, difference, the peak, the current rating, the change and the months stay. The builder applies it, and `ai::input::parse::parse_for_coach` applies it to every stored input the report job, the batch planner and the chat read. The validator accepts a null `kind` on a sufficient trajectory and `withheld` on the rating row only. The coach's prompts are `report/report.v5.md` and `chat/system.v7.md`: no word gives the rating a direction. The web's headline states the peak, the current rating and the change in one form, and the then | now table's rating row shows both averages and "Not called" with the difference. `compute`, `contract`, the web's `computeTrajectory` and `cli lab trajectory` are unchanged and still give `kind`. Accuracy, blunders per game, score and the weaknesses keep their verdicts. A report or chat answer written before this version keeps its text until the next report replaces it.
- Why: of 700 simulated players whose strength never changed, the trajectory told 44%, 62% and 78% that their rating was falling at 60, 120 and 250 rated games, against a bound of 15%, and 44% and 30% that it was rising at 60 and 120 games: a rating carries over from game to game (lag-1 correlation 0.97), so the bootstrap's interval is six to thirteen times too narrow, and the "then" window sits on a selected peak (`lab/experiments/EXP-0015/decision.md`). `backend/crates/coaching-input/tests/trajectory.rs` pins the switch to its `results.json`; EXP-0030 is the challenger that can turn it back on.
- ADRs: 0116 (the switch and where it cuts), 0112 (the precedent), 0105 (the version policy), 0109 (the arena).
- Benchmark: the release arena against `algo-4.0.1` (4.78 CPU-hours planned) finds no clear difference on any metric of EXP-0001 (recall at 2 at 80 games 0.633 on both sides, coverage 0.775; pilot rerun in 1,360 seconds), EXP-0002 (worst coverage 0.835, coverage at the peers' rate 0.797), EXP-0007 (the same 9 of 15 tags trusted), EXP-0008 (the version's node count is the one its rule chooses), EXP-0009 (mean split-half reliability 0.465, no key whose fall precedes climbing) or EXP-0015 (told "falling" 0.443, 0.621 and 0.784, unchanged, since the lab reads the rule before the cut). `bench/baseline.json` stands. The replay goldens change only in their header: no replay fixture account has 20 rated games in a time class, so no replayed trajectory carries a verdict. The lab's golden responses are byte-identical.

## algo-4.0.0+51733d23895f

2026-11-24. Every position is searched at 1 million nodes instead of 300,000, the count EXP-0008 chose (#128).

- What changed: `engine::ENGINE_NODES` is 1,000,000 and `ANALYSIS_PROTOCOL` is `sf19-n1m-v1`, with one thread, 16 MB of hash and `ucinewgame` before every search, as measured. The enrichment's MultiPV probe keeps 300,000 nodes under its own `enrich::protocol::MULTIPV_NODES` (the manifest's `facts.enrichment.multipv_nodes`), so `sf19-n300k-mpv3-v1` still says what it searched. Stored games keep their protocol and labels; the chat now reads a game's engine line under its own `analysis_protocol` and its cached eval falls back to `engine::EARLIER_ANALYSIS_PROTOCOLS`. `scheduler::lag::DEFAULT_CPU_MS_PER_GAME` is 39,000 ms. The TS reference worker mirrors the protocol.
- Why: against **Stockfish 19** at 30 million nodes on 160 rated rapid Lichess games, 1 million disagrees on 0.0246 of moves fewer than 300,000, 95% [0.0197, 0.0296], and on 0.0221 fewer [0.0115, 0.0327] against 100 million nodes on 40 of them, both wholly above the tolerance of 0.01 (`lab/experiments/EXP-0008/decision.md`, section 9, rule 1). 7.45% of move labels change, mostly fewer inaccuracies, and game accuracy falls by 0.42 of a point on average. The engine costs 39.1 CPU-seconds a game instead of 11.8: a 300-game import is about 3.3 CPU-hours, about 49 minutes on 4 engines.
- ADRs: 0113 (the protocol, the stored games, the MultiPV count), 0007 (the pinned protocol), 0105 (the version policy), 0109 (the arena).
- Benchmark: the release arena against `algo-3.0.0` finds EXP-0008 better on its primary (the version ships the count section 9 chooses; both contrasts unchanged at 0.012 and 0.025, re-run from the evaluation cache re-keyed to the engine crate's new digest), and no clear difference on any metric of EXP-0002 (worst coverage 0.835, coverage at the peers' rate 0.797) or EXP-0007 (the same 9 of 15 tags trusted, every precision and recall unchanged); and none on EXP-0001 (recall at 2 at 80 games 0.633 on both sides, coverage 0.775; pilot rerun in 1,311 seconds). `bench/baseline.json` stands, since the bench searches at its own 12,000 nodes. The replay goldens change only in the protocol label and the version: the replay searches at 3,000 nodes, so no number moved.

## algo-2.0.0+383bff27f04f

2026-11-24. On synthetic players drawn from EXP-0002's model, the 80% ranges hold the true rate 78% to 83% of the time for players well above their peers (EXP-0006).

- What changed: `estimate_weakness` counts a player's recency-weighted data at their effective sample size, `(Σ w n)² / Σ w² n` chances, divided by the Pearson dispersion of the per-game counts (clamped to [1, 10]), and the band prior is worth at most two of the player's own games (`min(n0[key], 2 × n / games_in_window)`). It is EXP-0006's challenger, `PriorRule::CHALLENGER`, now `PriorRule::CHAMPION`; the rule of 1.x is `PriorRule::ALGO_1` (`ALGO_VARIANT=algo-1`). W5's flat prior, the timeline's posteriors and the ranking rule are unchanged. Only `profile.source_digest` moved, and the replay goldens: every `weakness_estimates` row's counts, posterior and interval, and so priorities and states built on them.
- Why: EXP-0002 measured 12 of 20 key × sample-size coverages outside [0.75, 0.85] for players two to eight times their peers' rate in odds, down to 0.10 for W4.missed at 30 games (#13). EXP-0006 decided "ship": on fresh seeds every one of the challenger's 20 coverages lies inside the band (the worst 0.828, W5 at 30 games; D 0.028 [0.022, 0.043], 97.5%), against the champion's 0.103 (D 0.697); the coverage at the peers' rate moves from 0.874 to 0.804; credible estimates for players at or below their peers' rate rise by 0.0089 [0.0086, 0.0093] (80%), inside the registered margin of 0.01 with 0.0007 to spare, most for W4.missed (0.006 to 0.029) and W7.traps (0.001 to 0.017); Tier 1 recall at 2 is unchanged (28 of 45 on both sides) and the smoke suite's CI rule finds no regression. #13's own case (W1 against peers at 0.0008) is covered 0.766 [0.738, 0.791] at 30 games, below 0.80 and not shown inside the band, so #13 stays open (EXP-0025). The generator's peer rates, chances per game and game-to-game spread are assumptions; the ranges of real players are Tier 2's to measure.
- ADRs: 0111 (the prior rule and its variants), 0105 (the version policy), ADR-R2-5 (the posterior it amends).
- Benchmark: smoke suite, before and after: coverage of the 80% intervals 0.65 to 0.80 (60 intervals); recall at 2 0.6, top-1 precision 0.643, plan hits 0.6, top-2 agreement 0.927, clean false alarms 0 of 1, mean games to a detection 42, all unchanged. A smoke run of this version (521 seconds, 8 engines) gives EXP-0006's side-by-side challenger's scorecard field for field and rebuilt `bench/baseline.json`; `cli lab profile` of the contract fixture under this version equals `algo-1.1.1` under `ALGO_VARIANT=challenger`, estimate for estimate. The replay goldens and the lab's golden responses are regenerated.

## algo-1.0.0+fd96e117c013

2026-11-24. The first named version: the algorithm as it stood on `main` at 0d1afa6, with nothing changed in its output.

- What it is: **Stockfish 19** (`sf_19`, the pinned `stockfish-linux-x86-64-universal` build) at 300,000 nodes per position, protocol `sf19-n300k-v1`, one thread and 16 MB of hash, `ucinewgame` before every search; enrichment `enr-v1` (MultiPV 3 at 300,000 nodes on at most 30 critical plies, threats at 50,000 nodes); the **Syzygy** 3-4-5 set; **Maia 2** (`maia2==0.11.0`, model `rapid`) when `MAIA_URL` is set; player game metrics v3; the timeline detector `tl-v1`; the ranking rule `rank-v1` (ADR 0101: an 80% range above the benchmark and at least 0.02 points a game, 30 games); coaching input `coaching-input-v3` and `weekly-input-v1`; the trainer `trainer-v1` (ADR 0090) and the focus `focus-v1` (ADR 0091); the prompt sets and the models `claude-sonnet-5-5` and `claude-haiku-5-5` (API `2026-10-21`). `manifest.json` holds every value and digest.
- What changed: nothing a replay can see. `Threads 1` and `Hash 16` are now sent to every engine instead of being left to **Stockfish**'s defaults, which are the same values; `backend/crates/engine/tests/uci_audit.rs` shows the evaluations are identical with and without them.
- Why: so every stored recommendation names the rules that produced it (ADR 0104).
- ADRs: 0104 (the four layers and the version policy), 0105 (the manifest, the stamping and the replay suite), 0101 (the ranking rule).
- Benchmark: the baseline. Tier 1 smoke suite at 12,000 nodes per search (`docs/benchmark.md`): recall at 2 60%, top-1 precision 60%, clean controls with a rank-1 weakness 0 of 1, 80% intervals holding the true rate 60% (20 intervals), drills that train the flaw 60%, mean games to a top-2 detection 45, same data same weaknesses yes, top-2 overlap without 10% of the games 100%.
