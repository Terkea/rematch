## 1. Question

Among anonymised Lichess rapid players below 2000 who played for six months, does a fall in one of the rates Rematch measures, from one three-month window to the next, come before further climbing, counted from a rating that has had a month to catch up and to forget the exposure games' luck? Concretely: a 1400 player whose share of games with an early decisive error (W7.traps, "Early blunders") fell from October to December 2025 against January to March 2026; is that player rated higher at the end of June 2026 than at the end of April 2026, by more than an otherwise similar player, at the same level, whose rate did not fall?

These are candidates to teach, never causes. The question is narrower than EXP-0009's: a one-off improvement the rating has fully caught up with by the end of April reads zero here by construction. EXP-0009 is the reason for every change from its design (its `decision.md`, "Why the control failed" and "Next, item 1"; issue #131).

## 2. Hypotheses

For each quantity q of section 4, β_q is the expected rating change from the end of the gap month (April 2026) to the end of June 2026, in Elo, per observed standard deviation of improvement in q from window F1 to window F2, adjusted as in section 8 (the level adjusted is the mean of the two windows).

- H0: β_q = 0 for every q.
- H1: β_q ≥ 14 Elo per true SD of the improvement (14 × √reliability per observed SD) for at least one of the eight keys.

Rival explanations that would give the same numbers, and how each is handled:

- Shared luck between the exposure and the outcome's baseline (EXP-0009's flaw). A player who did well by chance in F2's games has lower rates there and a higher rating at F2's end, which then drifts back. Here the outcome starts a month later, when the rating has forgotten part of that luck: how much depends on the games played in April (section 4). The residual is negative and is estimated in advance from EXP-0009 (section 4; for accuracy −1.5 Elo per observed SD at 20 rapid games in April, −0.7 at 40). It works against "precedes climbing" and against the control, which the power analysis includes.
- A stable level the rating has not caught up with. EXP-0009's level family showed that a better level predicts later climbing (accuracy +6.2, W7.prep +5.9, W7.traps +4.4 Elo per observed SD). A model that adjusts only for the first window's level leaks some of that into the change: q₂ given a noisy q₁ still carries the level. By the critic's estimate that is about +0.8 to +1.0 Elo per observed SD for accuracy and W7.prep (`review.md`, B2), a positive bias towards "precedes climbing". The primary therefore adjusts for the window-mean level q̄ = (q₁ + q₂)/2. Its noise is uncorrelated with the change's noise when the two windows are equally noisy. Then the remaining leak is small and negative: about −6% of the minimum for accuracy, by the critic's algebra, against +33% for the F1-level model. When the windows' noise differs, the change and q̄ share (s₂ − s₁)/2 of it, so a sensitivity weights q̄ by each window's inverse binomial variance (section 8; `review.md`, N6). The F1-level model is a sensitivity too. So the shared luck biases the primary down, and the level biases it slightly down or not at all.
- Rating catch-up: after the gap, most of a catch-up to a change made in F2 is in the baseline; what the outcome sees is a change that keeps going. That is what section 9 claims, and no more.
- Regression to the mean in the rating: the F1 end rating, the change in the history month (September 2025) and from its end to the end of F1 are covariates; selection reads no rating change.
- Regression to the mean in the rate: the window-mean level of q is a covariate of every change model.
- New or returning accounts: at least 20 rapid games in September 2025, the history month (ADR-R2-11's activity rule, as EXP-0009 applied it to July).
- Reverse causation through opponents: the lag is the gap month plus the outcome; the change in the mean opponent gap is in the sensitivity model.
- Time-control switching: the rapid share in F1 and the rapid games in each window are covariates.
- Pool-wide rating drift: every player is in the same months; it is in the intercept.
- Who asks for analysis: only games with Lichess's `[%eval]` are measured, and the analysed share in F1 and in F2 is a covariate. The claim is for this population.
- Survivorship into F2: eligibility needs 30 analysed games in January to March, so it keeps players who carried on; quitting depends on F1's results. The flow from F1-eligible to F2-eligible is reported by F1 rating band and F1 activity tercile, and staying into F2 is modelled on the players-file F1 terms (section 8). Players who leave before F2 are not observed, so their F1 level is not measured. The claim is for players active for six months.
- Attrition after F2: players without 5 rapid games in April and in June have no outcome; their share is reported, and staying is modelled.
- Noise in the rates: the minimum effect per observed SD is 14 × √r_L, with r_L the run's own lower 95% bound of the reliability (section 8), so noise cannot pass for a small effect.

## 4. Primary metric and ground truth

- Primary metric (exactly one family): β_q for each of the nine quantities, each on its own regression; Holm at 0.05 across the eight keys, the control tested on its own (section 8). The quantities are EXP-0009's: the eight rankable keys whose detectors read only the evaluations, the clocks, the board and the result (`bench::observe::EVAL_ONLY_KEYS`: W4.missed, W4.allowed, W5, W6, W7.traps, W7.prep, W9a, W9b), each as events over opportunities summed over the window's observed games (missing below 5 opportunities in a window), and the mean game accuracy, the positive control. W7.prep is the self-hosted value (the opening ends at ply 20 without an explorer).
- The months (section 7): history H = 2025-09; F1 = 2025-10 to 2025-12; F2 = 2026-01 to 2026-03; the gap month G = 2026-04; the outcome O = May and June 2026, read as the change from the end of April to the end of June.
- Ratings, from each month's first and last five rapid games in the players files:
  - R_end of a month: the mean rating after its last five rapid games; R_start: the mean rating before its first five. A month needs 5 rapid games for its own R_end or R_start.
  - R_end of a window: the mean rating after the window's last five rapid games by time, taken from its last month and, if that month has fewer than five, from the months before it in the window. R_end(F1) is defined for every eligible player (30 analysed games in F1).
  - The outcome: y = R_end(2026-06) − R_end(2026-04); both months need 5 rapid games (otherwise attrition).
- The baseline, and why it is the gap month and not month-mean ratings. R_end(F2) carries the luck of F2's games, the same games that produce the exposure. A simple model of a rating's memory (`power.json`, `memory`): if a rating keeps a share φ of its error after each game (EXP-0015's Glicko-2 simulation measured 0.97; an established account at Lichess's lowest rating deviation is nearer 0.98), then of the luck held at F2's end:
  - EXP-0009's outcome, R_end(next month) − R_end(F2), carries −0.46 to −0.84 of it (φ 0.97, 20 to 60 rapid games a month);
  - the gap month's end ratings, R_end(June) − R_end(April), carry −0.38 to −0.16 at φ 0.97 and −0.37 to −0.27 at 0.98, because the baseline keeps only φ^(games in April). At 20 games a month the gap is no better than EXP-0009's outcome (−0.38 against −0.46 at φ 0.97, −0.37 against −0.33 at 0.98); it helps for players who play the gap month, which is why the sizing step reads April's activity and stops the run if it is low. EXP-0009's own players played a median of 121 rapid games in October 2026 (`dev/exp09_activity.json`); at 120 games a month the gap carries −0.03 at φ 0.97 and −0.09 at 0.98, against EXP-0009's −0.97 and −0.91;
  - month-mean ratings after the gap, mean over May and June − mean over April, carry −0.36 to −0.46: a month's mean starts at its first game and keeps more of the luck than its end;
  - a month-mean outcome with no gap still has F2's end as its baseline, and a mean over F2 is built from the exposure games.

  The gap month with end ratings is chosen: it is the only one of the two that takes the baseline away from the exposure games, and it needs no new cohort output. A two-month gap (the baseline at the end of May, the outcome June alone) was also evaluated (`power.json`, design B): it carries about a third of the luck at 20 games, but with one month of outcome a lasting link has half the Elo, and the control's power is lower (0.54 against 0.65 at w = 0.5, 20 games, 7 Elo per true SD over two months); it is not chosen.

  The same model's warning about the signal: the rating catches up to a real change at the rate it forgets luck, so the gap shrinks the catch-up still to come by the same factor as the bias. It removes most of the catch-up and most of the shared luck together, and keeps a change that continues after F2.

- The expected residual luck bias, registered per quantity (`power.json`, `simulation.sizing.bias`). It is EXP-0009's primary minus its adjusted estimate, the estimate with September's own rating change removed: accuracy −3.16, W5 −2.48, W6 −2.14, W7.traps −1.50, W4.allowed −1.30, W7.prep −1.22, W4.missed −0.90, W9b −0.46 and W9a −0.23 Elo per observed SD. That is multiplied by the memory ratio, then by 1/√3. The memory ratio is the mean over players of this design's luck share, φ^g_A (φ^(2 g_J) − 1) with g_A April's and g_J June's rapid games, divided by EXP-0009's mean share φ^g₀ − 1, measured on its own players' October games (`dev/exp09_activity.json`: 16,206 players, median 121 rapid games in October, 10th percentile 26, 6.8% below 20). A mean of per-player shares weights the low-games tail by its size, which a median does not (`review.md`, N1 and N5). The 1/√3 is there because only about the last month of a three-month window's games is still in F2's end rating, which is the less favourable end of the critic's range of √3 to 3. The gate uses the worse of φ 0.97 and 0.98. At the plan, with EXP-0009's October standing in for April and for each of May and June, the ratio is 0.11 at φ 0.97 and 0.17 at φ 0.98, and the bias for accuracy is −0.31. The shift also removes September's catch-up, so it is an estimate taken from development data. A wrong bias model is covered by the ×1.5 and ×2 sensitivities.
- The positive control under this baseline. Accuracy's lasting link is expected at 5 to 10 Elo per true SD over the two outcome months. Two development-data readings support that range. EXP-0009's adjusted model read +3.0 per observed SD at a reliability of 0.067, about 11.7 per true SD over one month, and that model removes September's luck and catch-up together. Its level family read 7.3 per true SD of the level over two months. With the bias above, at w = 0.6, the control holds with probability 0.86, 0.99 and 1.00 at 5, 7 and 10 Elo per true SD. The sizing step requires 0.80 at 7 after the bias. The control is read on the primary, the same model as the keys, so that it vouches for the keys' sensitivity under the same bias. Reading it on the adjusted model (option (b) of the review) would show that the data can see a link without the luck, but not that the keys' model can, so that reading does not decide. If the control fails, the result is outcome 3.
- EXP-0009's results are used here as development data only, to justify the baseline, set the bias and plan the power; none of this experiment's months has been read.
- Ground truth: the players' Lichess rapid ratings in the monthly dumps (CC0, many thanks to Lichess and its players).
- Not measured, and why: the nine keys that need the enrichment (W1, W2, W3.shallow, W3.deep, W8.basics, W8.advanced, W9c, W10, W11), at about 13,400 CPU-hours (section 6); per-band reads beyond the exploratory ones; which key fell first; the catch-up already in the April rating.
- Secondary metrics, none of which decides:
  - confirmatory: the level family, y = R_end(2026-06) − R_end(F1) on the F1 level of q, oriented, Holm across the eight keys;
  - sensitivity: the primary with the F1 level instead of the window mean (EXP-0009's model, with its level leak); the primary with q̄ weighted by each window's inverse binomial variance (from the opportunities); the primary with the post-exposure terms added (R_end(F2) − R_end(F1), R_end(2026-04) − R_end(F2), the change in the mean opponent gap / 100); the primary with inverse-probability-of-staying weights (staying into F2 and into the outcome); the primary on players with at least 30 rapid games in April;
  - descriptive: the no-gap outcome, R_end(2026-04) − R_end(F2), on the primary's exposure and terms (EXP-0009's flawed design on these players, to show what the gap took away); the contemporaneous change, R_end(F2) − R_end(F1), with the opponent gap change;
  - exploratory: the blunder rate on the primary's model; the primary within each band; the primary by tercile of April's rapid games (the bias should shrink with more games); whether the change predicts staying.

## 5. The minimum effect that matters

- Minimum effect: 14 Elo of rating change over the outcome (end of April to end of June) per standard deviation of the true improvement from F1 to F2; per observed SD, the unit the coefficient is in, 14 × √r_L, with r_L the run's own lower 95% bound of the exposure's split-half reliability (section 8).
- Why this size: 14 Elo against an equal opponent is about 0.02 points a game, ADR 0101's ranking floor (`RANK_MIN_EXCESS_PER_GAME`), the smallest gain the ranking already treats as worth a player's attention; a standard deviation of the true change is a unit every key shares. It is EXP-0009's number, but not the same unit: the true change here is between two three-month means about three months apart, not between consecutive months, and it is likely larger. Spearman-Brown assumes the true-change variance stays fixed when the windows grow; if it grows, the reliability is underestimated (conservative for the read), and 14 Elo per SD is a bigger change in the rate than in EXP-0009. EXP-0009 read its minimum at the point reliability, whose standard error (about 0.015 on the Spearman-Brown value) could make "below the minimum effect" too easy; the run's lower bound closes that.

## 6. Power analysis

- Method: a simulation of section 9's rule end to end (`power.py`, 20,000 replicates a cell, seed `seed("EXP-0029", "power")`; it replaces stage 1's closed form, `review.md`, B1). Each replicate draws:
  - each quantity's split-half correlation at its n, around the assumed true three-month reliability (Fisher z), then the run's lower 95% bound, Spearman-Brown and the 0.01 floor, which together set its minimum, 14 × √r_L;
  - the nine coefficients, equicorrelated at ρ = 0.3. The keys share players and games, and EXP-0009's results do not hold the correlation; round 2 showed that 0 and 0.6 change nothing. Each coefficient sits at its true effect × √(true reliability), plus the registered bias (section 4), with the planned standard error;
  - then Holm across the eight keys, the control's own test, the simultaneous interval at 1 − 0.05/8, and the three outcomes, with outcome 2 needing the five registered keys.
- The assumed true reliability: EXP-0009's point estimate, through Spearman-Brown at the measured k, with the run's own lower bound as the only penalty. The lower bound as the truth is the sensitivity.
- Inputs: EXP-0009's committed `results.json` gives:
  - the reliabilities: W7.prep 0.019, W4.missed 0.035, W9b 0.040, W7.traps 0.048, W4.allowed 0.051, W6 0.056, accuracy 0.067, W5 0.087, W9a 0.131;
  - the standard error: EXP-0009's HC3 standard error per quantity × √(n_0009 / n) × 1.34, how much wider a two-month outcome's error is than a one-month one's (the upper quartile of EXP-0009's level-family standard error over its primary's, 0.94 to 1.71);
  - the players: EXP-0009's players per unit of hash for each quantity × 0.7, the planned density. The stand-in below suggests more (one three-month window alone keeps 1.40 times EXP-0009's players per unit of hash), but the second window and the rating rule cut that, so 0.7 stays.
  `dev/exp09_activity.json` gives:
  - k = E_0009[1/m] / E[1/min(m, 120)] = 0.0481 / 0.0182 = 2.64, measured. EXP-0009's m is each feature month's analysed games capped at 40. Here m is the stand-in window's (August to October 2026, for players with 20 rapid games in July and 30 analysed games in the three months; median 58, 10th percentile 33). Stage 1's k = 3 assumed equal counts; the harmonic ratio is lower (`review.md`, S4 and N3);
  - games read per player and window: E[min(m, 144)] = 70.4, and 62.7 and 55.7 at the fallback caps (96 and 72);
  - the memory ratio of section 4.
- Outcome 2's list was fixed at round 2 from the simulation and does not change: W4.allowed, W5, W6, W7.traps and W9a. The gate tests these five, not a recomputed list (`review.md`, N4). W7.prep and W4.missed read "below the minimum effect" too rarely, because their run lower bounds sit at or near the floor, and W9b almost never does. Outcome 2 says nothing about those three (section 9).
- The target, which the sizing step tests at the size it chooses:

  - (b) each of them alone at 14 Elo per true SD reads "precedes climbing" with probability at least 0.80;
  - (c) the control holds with probability at least 0.80 at 7 Elo per true SD;
  - all after the bias at the worse of φ 0.97 and 0.98.
- The size: for each per-window cap in the fallback order (144 read and 120 kept, then 96 and 80, then 72 and 60), the size is the largest w in 0.1, …, 0.6 whose projected cost fits all three caps: games within 5,550,000 (150% of the budget); CPU-hours within 25 (streaming as measured, plus 1.7 CPU-hours a million games, twice); wall time within 24 hours (0.91 hours a million games, twice, plus 8 hours for checks and the write-up). A smaller w would save a few CPU-hours, but the streaming and the maintainer's days cost the same at any size, so the size is the largest that fits (`review.md`, N2). If the target fails there, the next cap in the order is tried at its own largest fitting w. The kill rule applies only when every cap fails, or none fits (`review.md`, R3-1).
- Result at the plan (`power.json`, `simulation.sizing`): the plan uses EXP-0009's activity as a stand-in for April's and for the window's, density 0.7 and the error ratio 1.34. The size is w = 0.6 with the first cap: about 24,000 players observed and about 22,700 with an outcome; 3.38 million games; 18.5 CPU-hours in all; about 14 hours of wall time after the sizing commit. ## 7. Design

- Unit of analysis: the player, one row each; games are nested in players and never counted as replicates.
- Months: H = 2025-09 (players file only: the activity rule and the history covariates); F1 = 2025-10, 2025-11, 2025-12 and F2 = 2026-01, 2026-02, 2026-03 (with `--games`); G = 2026-04 and 2026-06 (players files only). 2026-05 is not streamed: the outcome uses end ratings, and May's games already move the rating June's end reports. Issue #131 names 2025-10 to 2026-06; September 2025 is added as the history month (`review.md`, S5), and every month is disjoint from EXP-0009's 2026-07 to 2026-10.
- The cohort (ADR 0114): `lichess-db cohort --salt 80b7ed61df63b5e94e96089d100887cef8465b13028260352639573bee9ae5d4` (EXP-0009's salt, `sha256("EXP-0009/cohort/salt")`, so a player has the same pseudonym and hash fraction in both experiments) on the standard rated dumps of the nine months, from `https://database.lichess.org/standard/`. Each file's URL, size and verified SHA-256 go into its `meta.json` and into `inputs.sha256` as external inputs before the sizing step. Nothing per player is committed.
- EXP-0009's whole cohort is excluded by its hash range: EXP-0009 drew its pilot from [0.9, 1) and its confirmatory slice from [0, 0.3), so this experiment uses only players with a hash fraction in [0.3, 0.9). No player EXP-0009 could have followed is here, whether or not they passed its eligibility. The confirmatory slice is [0.3, 0.3 + w), w from the sizing step; there is no pilot on new months.
- Eligibility, in order, from the players files (the count each step leaves is reported, by F1 rating band and F1 activity tercile for the step into F2, and the final cohort by band): at least 20 rapid games in 2025-09; at least 30 rapid games with `[%eval]` in each of F1 and F2 (summed over the window's three months); R_end(F1) below 2000. Then from the games: per player and window, the first 144 such games by `sha256(salt ‖ "game:" ‖ id)` are observed (or the smaller cap the sizing step chose), games that are not standard chess from the start position or that observe with an error are dropped, and the first 120 left are kept; a player needs 30 kept in each window. The outcome needs at least 5 rapid games in 2026-04 and in 2026-06; a player without them is in the attrition analysis only.
- Seeds: `seed("EXP-0029", "power")` and `seed("EXP-0029", "sizing")`, in `power.py` only. The analysis draws nothing at random; every interval is analytic. The results record the namespace `EXP-0029` for the arena.
- Development and confirmatory data: this experiment's development data are EXP-0009's committed results (the baseline's justification, the bias and the planned power) and its players files, measured by `dev/exp09_activity.py` into `dev/exp09_activity.json`. Those files give October's activity, E_0009[1/m], and a stand-in for a three-month window. EXP-0009 had already read them, and their checksums are in its `inputs.sha256`. EXP-0009's players and months are not otherwise used. The new months are read first by the sizing step (counts, April's and June's activity, and the outcome's SD, blind to the exposures) and then by the confirmatory run.
- Held fixed: the measured algorithm version (section 3); the start position's search at 1 million nodes, one thread, 16 MB of hash; Lichess's own evaluations for every other position.
- Leakage controls: the exposure comes from F1 and F2, the outcome from the ends of April and June, the covariates from H and F1 (and F2's game counts and analysed share); nothing after F2 enters a feature, a covariate or a selection rule except the outcome's activity rule, handled as attrition. The run counts the games between two observed players of the slice (whose rating changes share a game) and the games with an opponent outside it.
- Stage 2, in this order, and nothing streamed before step 2 is committed:
  1. `analysis.py` is written from EXP-0009's and imports its frozen definitions where they are the same. It is tested against a synthetic cohort with a known truth (`lab/tests/exp0029_synthetic.py`, `lab/tests/test_exp0029_analysis.py`), covering the windowing, the rating definitions of section 4, the window-mean level, the Fisher-z bound, the reads, the outcomes, the budget cap and the sizing step's measurements. `lichess-db cohort` needs no change. The gap month and the history month are players files only (no `--games`), which the command already writes. `--hash-range` is not built: with `--player-rate 0.9`, [0, 0.3) is dropped on reading, and the six months' games take about 56 GB (EXP-0009's 9.7 to 10.4 GB a month at a rate of 1, times 0.9), with about 3 GB of players files.   2. A fresh critic reviews them (`review.md`, part one, continued); its fixes are committed; section 10 states `analysis.py`'s sha256. From this commit on, `analysis.py` changes only as a deviation.

## 8. Analysis plan

- Model: for each quantity q, an ordinary least squares regression with HC3 standard errors and normal (z) intervals (statsmodels), one row per player with q in both windows. Every change model adjusts for terms measured before the outcome and not caused by the exposure's rating effect: the window-mean level q̄ = (q₁ + q₂)/2 (oriented), (R_end(F1) − 1500)/100, R_end(F1) − R_end(2025-09), R_end(2025-09) − R_start(2025-09), log(1 + rapid games) in H, F1 and F2, the rapid share of the player's games in F1, the analysed share (rapid games with `[%eval]` over rapid games) in F1 and in F2, and log(1 + opportunities) of q in F1 and in F2 (which also carry an unequal noise between the windows).
  - primary: y = R_end(2026-06) − R_end(2026-04) on the standardised improvement Δq = q₂ − q₁ (oriented so a positive value is better: a lower rate, a higher accuracy) and those terms;
  - F1-level (sensitivity): the primary with q₁ instead of q̄;
  - adjusted (sensitivity): the primary plus R_end(F2) − R_end(F1), R_end(2026-04) − R_end(F2) and the change in the mean (opponent − own) rating of the observed games from F1 to F2, divided by 100;
  - no-gap (descriptive): y = R_end(2026-04) − R_end(F2), the primary's exposure and terms;
  - contemporaneous (descriptive): y = R_end(F2) − R_end(F1), the same terms and the opponent gap change;
  - level (secondary, confirmatory): y = R_end(2026-06) − R_end(F1) on the standardised, oriented F1 level, with (R_end(F1) − 1500)/100, R_end(F1) − R_end(2025-09), R_end(2025-09) − R_start(2025-09), log(1 + rapid games) in H and F1, the F1 rapid share, the F1 analysed share, the F1 opportunities and the F1 opponent gap / 100.
- Reliability: for each quantity and model, on the players the model used: q on each window's odd and even observed games by game key, the change (or level) on each half, their correlation ρ over the n players with both halves, and r = 2ρ / (1 + ρ). Its 95% interval comes from Fisher's z on ρ (z ± 1.96 / √(n − 3), n the players with both halves), mapped back and through Spearman-Brown; r_L is the lower end, floored at 0.01. The minimum effect per observed SD is 14 × √r_L. β / √r and β / √r_L are reported as the disattenuated effect.
- Effect size: β_q in Elo per observed SD with its 80%, 95% and 97.5% intervals and the simultaneous interval at 1 − 0.05/8 (99.375%).
- Multiple comparisons: Holm at 0.05 across the eight keys within each family. The control is not in the keys' family: it is a check of the design, not a candidate to teach, and putting it in would loosen the next key's threshold once it is rejected (`review.md`, M1); it is tested on its own at 0.05, two-sided. Only the primary decides.
- Reads, per key: "precedes climbing" when Holm rejects and β > 0; "precedes falling" when Holm rejects and β < 0; "below the minimum effect" when the simultaneous interval lies inside (−14 × √r_L, 14 × √r_L); "undetermined" otherwise; "not estimable" with fewer than 30 players. The control holds when its own test rejects and β > 0.
- Attrition: staying into F2 (30 analysed games in F2) on the players-file F1 terms (the F1 end rating, the history month's change and the change to F1's end, rapid games in H and F1, the F1 rapid and analysed shares), and staying into the outcome (5 rapid games in both April and June) on the primary's exposure and terms, linear probability models with HC3; and the primary re-fitted by weighted least squares with weights 1 / (p̂_F2 · p̂_outcome), each from a logistic model and floored at 0.05. Sensitivities only.
- Diagnostics: no Bayesian model. A quantity with fewer than 30 players is not estimable; a logistic fit that fails is reported as failed.
- Exclusions and missing data: decided in section 7; a key below 5 opportunities in a window is missing for that key only; complete cases per quantity. No outlier removal.

## 9. Decision rule

Measurement on one version, read on the primary family (section 8), in this order:

1. **Candidates to teach:** at least one of the eight keys reads "precedes climbing". We publish them as candidates, never as causes ("players whose rate fell went on climbing after their rating had a month to catch up"), and register a per-band comparison experiment of re-ordered trainability rows on new months, with this result as its development data. `trainability` does not change here. The F1-level sensitivity is reported beside it; a key that reads "precedes climbing" only there is a level artefact and not a candidate. Holm caps at 5% the chance that any key with no link reads "precedes climbing", so a candidate can be a false positive: each is reported with its raw and its Holm p, and the follow-up experiment on new months is its replication before anything changes (`review.md`, S2a-5).
2. **No further climbing from these keys:** no key reads "precedes climbing"; every key of outcome 2's list (W4.allowed, W5, W6, W7.traps, W9a: section 6, before any data) reads "below the minimum effect"; and the control holds. `trainability` keeps its rows, and the site says: on Lichess's analysed rapid games, among players active for six months, a fall in these five weaknesses over three months did not come before further climbing of as much as 14 Elo per SD, once the rating had a month to catch up. It does not say these weaknesses don't matter: a one-off improvement the rating caught up with by the end of April is in the baseline and reads zero here, and W4.missed, W7.prep and W9b are not covered, because the design cannot read them below the minimum.
3. **Need more data, for the second time:** anything else. `trainability` keeps its rows; there is no third follow-up under this rule (EXP-0009, section 9); the site keeps "no data-driven teaching order yet"; the question goes to the learning experiments (v0.6).

Stage 2 is out of scope at its price (section 6). This departs from ADR 0106's measurement rule for the reason EXP-0009 gave: there is no band a link should lie in, and the control guards against reading an insensitive design as a null.

### Stopping rules

- Early stopping: none. The run is one pass once the months are streamed.
- Futility: the sizing step (section 6): if no size reaches the target within the caps, no game is observed (the kill rule of section 0).
- Budget: the run aborts at the cap of section 0; an aborted run is a failed result, reported as one. The caps outside `analysis.py` stop the stage that passes them, and the decision says which.
