## 1. Question

Among anonymised Lichess rapid players below 2000, does a fall in one of the rates Rematch measures, from one three-month window to the next, come before a rise in rating over the months after, counted from a rating that has had a month to forget the exposure games' luck? Concretely: a 1400 player whose share of games with an early decisive error (W7.traps, "Early blunders") falls from October to December 2025 against January to March 2026; is that player rated higher at the end of June 2026 than at the end of April 2026, by more than an otherwise similar player whose rate did not fall?

These are candidates to teach, never causes. EXP-0009 is the reason for every change from its design (its `decision.md`, "Why the control failed" and "Next, item 1"; issue #131).

## 2. Hypotheses

For each quantity q of section 4, β_q is the expected rating change from the end of the gap month to the end of the outcome window, in Elo, per observed standard deviation of improvement in q from window F1 to window F2, adjusted as in section 8.

- H0: β_q = 0 for every q.
- H1: β_q ≥ 14 Elo per true SD of the improvement (14 × √reliability per observed SD) for at least one of the eight keys.

Rival explanations that would give the same numbers, and how each is handled:

- Shared luck between the exposure and the outcome's baseline (EXP-0009's flaw). A player who did well by chance in F2's games has lower rates there and a higher rating at F2's end, which then drifts back. EXP-0009 started its outcome at that rating, so the drift pulled every β towards negative. Here the outcome starts a month later (the gap month, section 4), when the rating has forgotten part of that luck. The rating forgets the catch-up to a real change at the same rate (section 4, "the baseline"), so the gap leaves a smaller negative bias and less catch-up, and keeps what lasts. The bias is still negative, so it works against "precedes climbing", never for it.
- Rating catch-up: a rating lags the skill, so a player who has got better rises over the next weeks whatever caused it. After the gap, most of a catch-up to a change made in F2 is already in the baseline; what the outcome sees is a change that keeps going. That is nearer the question than EXP-0009's lag was, though still not causal.
- Regression to the mean in the rating: the F1 end rating and the rating changes within October 2025 and from its end to the end of F1 are covariates; selection reads no rating change.
- Regression to the mean in the rate: the F1 level of q is a covariate of every change model.
- New or returning accounts: at least 20 rapid games in October 2025, the band reference's activity rule (ADR-R2-11). Without a month before it, a returning account with 20 games in October is in; a limit, stated.
- Reverse causation through opponents: the lag is the gap month plus the outcome; the change in the mean opponent gap is a covariate of the sensitivity model only.
- Time-control switching: the rapid share in F1 and the rapid games in each window are covariates.
- Pool-wide rating drift: every player is in the same months; it is in the intercept.
- Who asks for analysis: only games with Lichess's `[%eval]` are measured, and the analysed share in F1 and in F2 is a covariate. The claim is for this population.
- Attrition: players without 5 rapid games in the gap month and in the last outcome month have no outcome; their share is reported, and staying is modelled (section 8).
- Noise in the rates: the exposure is standardised by its observed SD, which is mostly noise for a rare key. The minimum effect per observed SD is 14 × √reliability, and the reliability is read at the lower end of its 95% interval (section 8), so noise cannot pass for a small effect.

## 4. Primary metric and ground truth

- Primary metric (exactly one family, Holm across its nine members): β_q for each of the nine quantities, each on its own regression. They are EXP-0009's: the eight rankable keys whose detectors read only the evaluations, the clocks, the board and the result (`bench::observe::EVAL_ONLY_KEYS`: W4.missed, W4.allowed, W5, W6, W7.traps, W7.prep, W9a, W9b), each as events over opportunities summed over the window's observed games (missing below 5 opportunities in a window), and the mean game accuracy, the positive control. W7.prep is the self-hosted value (the opening ends at ply 20 without an explorer).
- The windows (section 7): F1 = 2025-10 to 2025-12, F2 = 2026-01 to 2026-03, the gap month G = 2026-04, the outcome window O = 2026-04 to 2026-06, of which only the end of April and the end of June are read.
- The outcome: y = R_end(2026-06) − R_end(2026-04), the end rating of a month being the mean of the ratings after its last five rapid games, as in EXP-0009.
- The baseline, and why it is the gap month and not month-mean ratings. The flaw to fix: R_end(F2) carries the luck of F2's games, the same games that produce the exposure. A simple model of a rating's memory shows what each option does (`power.json`, `memory`): if a rating keeps a share φ of its error after each game (EXP-0015's Glicko-2 simulation measured a game-to-game correlation of 0.97; an established account at Lichess's lowest rating deviation forgets more slowly, about 0.98), then of the luck the rating holds at F2's end:
  - EXP-0009's outcome, R_end(next month) − R_end(F2), carries −0.46 to −0.84 of it (φ 0.97, 20 to 60 rapid games a month), all of it at the baseline;
  - the gap month's end ratings, R_end(O) − R_end(G), carry −0.38 to −0.16 at φ 0.97 and −0.37 to −0.27 at 0.98, falling as the gap month has more games, because the baseline keeps only φ^(games in G) of it (0.54 to 0.16 at φ 0.97). At 20 games a month the gap is no better than EXP-0009's one-month outcome (−0.38 against −0.46 at φ 0.97, −0.37 against −0.33 at 0.98), because a two-month outcome gives the rest of the luck longer to drain; the gain comes from players who play the gap month, which is why a sensitivity keeps only those with 30 rapid games in it;
  - month-mean ratings after the gap, mean over May and June − mean over April, carry −0.36 to −0.46: a mean over a month starts at the month's first game, so it keeps more of the luck than the month's end does, and the outcome's mean keeps less of it, which works the wrong way on both sides;
  - a month-mean outcome with no gap, mean over the next month − R_end(F2), still has F2's end as its baseline, the flaw itself; and a mean over F2 as the baseline is built from the exposure games by definition.

  So the gap month with end ratings is the option that takes the outcome's baseline away from the exposure games; month-mean ratings would move the luck around without removing it, and they would also need a new output from `lichess-db cohort` (a mean of every rapid rating in the month), where the gap month needs none. The gap month is chosen.

  What the same model says about the signal, which decides how the control should behave: the rating catches up to a real change of skill at the rate it forgets luck, so the gap shrinks the catch-up still to come by the same factor φ^(games in G) as it shrinks the bias. The gap does not make a catch-up easier to see; it removes most of the catch-up and most of the shared luck together, and keeps a change that continues after F2 (a player still improving, or a rating that lags more than one month of games, as EXP-0009's level family suggests: an August level predicted the change from August's end to October's end by 7.3 Elo per true SD of accuracy). That is the link worth knowing for a teaching order, and EXP-0009 called catch-up the main reason it could make no causal claim.

- The positive control under this baseline. Accuracy's improvement is expected to come before climbing by less than in a design that keeps the catch-up. EXP-0009's committed results bound it loosely: with September's own rating change in the model, accuracy read +3.0 Elo per observed SD at a reliability of 0.067, about 11.7 per true SD, which removes September's luck and September's catch-up together; its level family's 7.3 per true SD is a lasting link of a level, not of a change. We expect a lasting link for accuracy of 5 to 12 Elo per true SD. The control holds (reads "precedes climbing") with probability 0.83 at 7 and 0.46 at 5 Elo per true SD at the planned size (`power.json`, `control_sensitivity_at_w`). If it fails, the result is outcome 3, which keeps the rows; the design cannot then say whether catch-up ends within a month or the link is absent, and the secondaries below are there to tell those apart descriptively. EXP-0009's results are used here as development data only, to justify the baseline and to plan the power; none of this experiment's months has been read.
- Ground truth: the players' Lichess rapid ratings in the monthly dumps (CC0, many thanks to Lichess and its players).
- Not measured, and why: the nine keys that need the enrichment (W1, W2, W3.shallow, W3.deep, W8.basics, W8.advanced, W9c, W10, W11), which stage 2 would measure at a price of about 28,600 CPU-hours (section 6); per-band reads beyond the exploratory ones; which key fell first.
- Secondary metrics, none of which decides:
  - confirmatory: the level family, y = R_end(2026-06) − R_end(F1) on the F1 level of q, oriented so a positive β means a better level, Holm across nine (EXP-0009's level family over a longer lag);
  - descriptive: the no-gap outcome, R_end(G) − R_end(F2), on the same exposure and terms as the primary. It carries the shared luck and most of the catch-up, so it is EXP-0009's flawed design on these players; read beside the primary, it shows how much of a link the gap took away;
  - descriptive: the contemporaneous change, R_end(F2) − R_end(F1), on the same exposure, with the change in the opponent gap (the issue's climbers against stayers; partly mechanical for W5, W6 and accuracy);
  - sensitivity: the primary with the post-exposure terms added (R_end(F2) − R_end(F1), R_end(G) − R_end(F2), and the change in the mean opponent gap / 100); the primary with inverse-probability-of-staying weights; the primary on players with at least 30 rapid games in the gap month, where φ^(games in G) is at most 0.40 at φ 0.97;
  - exploratory: the blunder rate on the primary's model; the primary within each band (B0 to B3); the primary by tercile of the gap month's rapid games (the shared luck should shrink as the gap has more games); whether the change predicts staying.

## 5. The minimum effect that matters

- Minimum effect: 14 Elo of rating change over the outcome per standard deviation of the true improvement in the quantity; per observed SD, the unit the coefficient is in, 14 × √r_L, with r_L the exposure's split-half reliability at the lower end of its 95% interval, measured in the run (section 8).
- Why this size: EXP-0009's, unchanged, so the follow-up asks the same question. 14 Elo against an equal opponent is about 0.02 points a game, ADR 0101's ranking floor (`RANK_MIN_EXCESS_PER_GAME`), the smallest gain the ranking already treats as worth a player's attention; a standard deviation of the true change is a unit all keys share. EXP-0009 read its minimum at the point estimate of each reliability, whose uncertainty (a standard error of about 0.015 on the Spearman-Brown value, 0.008 on the half correlation) could make "below the minimum effect" easier than it should be; the lower bound closes that. The outcome here is two months of rating change, not one; the minimum stays 14 Elo, so the bar is, if anything, easier for a lasting link to clear and no easier for noise.

## 6. Power analysis

- Method: EXP-0009's closed form for the registered read (`power.py`, which writes `power.json`): the HC3 z-test at the simultaneous level 1 − 0.05/9 (Bonferroni standing in for Holm, slightly conservative). Two powers per quantity: of "precedes climbing" at a true 14 Elo per true SD (14 × √r per observed SD), and of "below the minimum effect" at 0. The target: at least six of the eight keys reach 0.80 in both, and the control reaches 0.80 for "precedes climbing" (what outcome 2 needs).
- Inputs, all from EXP-0009's committed `results.json` (development data; no new month read):
  - the reliability of each quantity's change at the lower end of its 95% interval: the half correlation ρ = r / (2 − r) behind EXP-0009's Spearman-Brown value r, its Fisher-z interval at the players the model used, and Spearman-Brown again, floored at 0.01. W4.missed (0.035), W7.prep (0.019) and W9b (0.040) fall to the floor at their lower bound; W7.traps 0.048 to 0.018, W4.allowed 0.051 to 0.022, W6 0.056 to 0.022, accuracy 0.067 to 0.038, W5 0.087 to 0.051, W9a 0.131 to 0.104;
  - Spearman-Brown for three times the games (k = 3: the windows are three months and the cap is 120 games a window, three times 40): W7.traps 0.053, W4.allowed 0.062, W6 0.063, accuracy 0.107, W5 0.139, W9a 0.258, and 0.029 for the three at the floor. As issue #131 warns, three times the games lifts a reliability of 0.05 only to about 0.14, and from the lower bound less than that;
  - the standard error: EXP-0009's confirmatory HC3 standard error per quantity, scaled by √(n_0009 / n) and by 1.34, how much wider a two-month outcome's error is than a one-month one's (the upper quartile, across the nine quantities, of EXP-0009's level-family standard error over its primary's on the same players: 0.94 to 1.71, median 1.24);
  - the players: EXP-0009's players per unit of hash for each quantity (W4.missed and W9b need 5 opportunities in both windows and are rarer), times 0.7, the planned density of this design's eligibility (30 analysed games in each of two three-month windows, against 10 in each of two months) relative to EXP-0009's. An assumption, replaced by the count before any game is observed (below).
- Result at the plan (`power.json`): the confirmatory slice is the hash range [0.3, 0.3 + w), and w = 0.5 is the smallest of 0.1, 0.2, …, 0.6 that reaches the target: about 20,000 players observed, 18,900 with an outcome, about 3.07 million games to observe. Six keys reach 0.80 in both powers there (W4.allowed 0.99/0.99, W5 1.00/1.00, W6 0.98/0.96, W7.prep 0.90/0.81, W7.traps 1.00/0.99, W9a 1.00/1.00) and accuracy 1.00. W4.missed (0.40/0.00) and W9b (0.07/0.00), whose reliability sits at the floor and whose players are few, cannot read "below the minimum effect" at any size this range offers, so outcome 2 needs all six others; under H0, the chance that all six read it is about 0.76 at w = 0.5 if they were independent.
- Sensitivity (`power.json`, `sensitivity`; 24 cells of k ∈ {2, 3}, density ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 1.0} and the error ratio ∈ {1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0}): the target is reached within [0.3, 0.9) in 10 of the 24. It is reached at every density with the ratio at 1.0, and at density 0.7 with the ratio up to 1.25 (k = 3); it fails at a ratio of 1.5 unless the density is 1.0 with k = 3, and at 2.0 everywhere. The design is sized close to what the hash range allows, and W7.prep is the key that decides it.
- The sizing step (stage 2, after the months are streamed and before any game is observed, blind to every exposure): from the players files alone, in [0.3, 0.9), (a) the count of players who pass the eligibility of section 7 up to the game rules, which sets the density; (b) their mean analysed rapid games per window, capped at 144, against EXP-0009's 25.6 per month, which sets k; (c) the SD of y among those with an outcome, against EXP-0009's 53.8, which replaces the error ratio. This reads the outcome's spread, never its link to any quantity, and it changes only w: a blinded re-estimate of nuisance parameters, which does not touch the error rates. w is then the smallest grid value at which `power.py`'s rule reaches the target with those three inputs and the same lower-bound reliabilities; it is written to `power.json` (`sizing`) and committed before any game is observed. If no w up to 0.6 reaches it, or the games at w exceed the budget, the kill rule of section 0 applies.
- Stage 2 (the nine enriched keys at 1 million nodes) is not in scope. Priced again as EXP-0009's decision asks, at this design: the median eval-only key, as the stand-in for keys whose reliability nobody has measured, needs about 10,960 players for 0.80 in both powers; at 240 games each (the cap) and 39.1 CPU-seconds a game (EXP-0008), about 28,600 CPU-hours, or about 18,300 at the planned mean of 154 games. That is more than six times EXP-0009's 4,560 and over 900 times the ceiling of 20 CPU-hours: the longer windows need more games per player and the lower-bound reliabilities more players. It does not run, and it is named with this price in the decision.

## 7. Design

- Unit of analysis: the player, one row each; games are nested in players and never counted as replicates.
- Months and windows: F1 = 2025-10, 2025-11, 2025-12; F2 = 2026-01, 2026-02, 2026-03; the gap month G = 2026-04; the outcome's end 2026-06. 2026-05 is not streamed: the outcome uses only end ratings, so May's games move the rating that June's end reports, and nothing of May is needed on its own. Nine months were named (#131); four three-month windows and a gap would need thirteen, so there is no separate history window: October 2025 plays its part (the activity rule and the earliest rating) inside F1. Every month is disjoint from EXP-0009's 2026-07 to 2026-10.
- The cohort (ADR 0114): `lichess-db cohort --salt 80b7ed61df63b5e94e96089d100887cef8465b13028260352639573bee9ae5d4` (EXP-0009's salt, `sha256("EXP-0009/cohort/salt")`, so a player has the same pseudonym and hash fraction in both experiments) on the standard rated dumps of the eight months, from `https://database.lichess.org/standard/`, with `--games` for the six feature months. Each file's URL, size and verified SHA-256 go into its `meta.json` and into `inputs.sha256` as external inputs before the sizing step. Nothing per player is committed.
- EXP-0009's whole cohort is excluded by its hash range: EXP-0009 drew its pilot from [0.9, 1) and its confirmatory slice from [0, 0.3), so this experiment uses only players with a hash fraction in [0.3, 0.9). No player EXP-0009 could have followed is here, whether or not they passed its eligibility, and no list of its pseudonyms is needed. The confirmatory slice is [0.3, 0.3 + w), w from section 6 (planned 0.5); there is no pilot on new months.
- Eligibility, in order, from the players files (the count each step leaves is reported, and the final cohort by band): at least 20 rapid games in 2025-10; at least 30 rapid games with `[%eval]` in each of F1 and F2 (summed over the window's three months); R_end(2025-12) below 2000. Then from the games: per player and window, the first 144 such games by `sha256(salt ‖ "game:" ‖ id)` are observed, games that are not standard chess from the start position or that observe with an error are dropped, and the first 120 left are kept; a player needs 30 kept in each window. The outcome needs at least 5 rapid games in 2026-04 and in 2026-06; a player without them is in the attrition analysis only.
- Seeds: none. Nothing is drawn at random; every interval is analytic. The results record the namespace `EXP-0029` for the arena.
- Development and confirmatory data: EXP-0009's committed results are this experiment's development data (the baseline's justification and the planned power); its players and months are not used. The new months are read only by the sizing step (counts and the outcome's SD, blind to the exposures) and then by the confirmatory run. Sections 2, 4, 5, 8 and 9 are committed before any new month is streamed.
- Held fixed: the measured algorithm version (section 3); the start position's search at 1 million nodes, one thread, 16 MB of hash; Lichess's own evaluations for every other position.
- Leakage controls: the exposure comes from F1 and F2, the outcome from the end of G and of June, the covariates from F1 (and F2's game counts and analysed share); nothing after F2 enters a feature, a covariate or a selection rule except the outcome's own activity rule, handled as attrition. The run counts the games of an observed player with an opponent outside the slice.
- Work for stage 2, not done here:
  - `analysis.py`, from EXP-0009's: three-month windows, the gap-month outcome, the lower-bound reliability, the exclusion by hash range, the secondaries above, and the sizing step (`analysis.py sizing`), with tests of the windowing and of the Fisher-z bound;

If the option is not built, the run uses `--player-rate 0.9` and drops [0, 0.3) when it reads, inside the 70 GB cap;

## 8. Analysis plan

- Model: for each quantity q, an ordinary least squares regression with HC3 standard errors and normal (z) intervals (statsmodels), one row per player with q in both windows. Every change model adjusts for terms measured before the outcome and not caused by the exposure's rating effect: the F1 level of q (oriented), (R_end(F1) − 1500)/100, R_end(F1) − R_end(2025-10), R_end(2025-10) − R_start(2025-10), log(1 + rapid games) in F1 and in F2, the rapid share of the player's games in F1, the analysed share (rapid games with `[%eval]` over rapid games) in F1 and in F2, and log(1 + opportunities) of q in F1 and in F2.
  - primary: y = R_end(2026-06) − R_end(2026-04) on the standardised improvement Δq (F1 to F2, oriented so a positive value is better: a lower rate, a higher accuracy) and those terms;
  - adjusted (sensitivity): the primary plus R_end(F2) − R_end(F1), R_end(2026-04) − R_end(F2) and the change in the mean (opponent − own) rating of the observed games from F1 to F2, divided by 100;
  - no-gap (descriptive): y = R_end(2026-04) − R_end(F2), the primary's exposure and terms;
  - contemporaneous (descriptive): y = R_end(F2) − R_end(F1), the same terms and the opponent gap change;
  - level (secondary, confirmatory): y = R_end(2026-06) − R_end(F1) on the standardised, oriented F1 level, with (R_end(F1) − 1500)/100, R_end(F1) − R_end(2025-10), R_end(2025-10) − R_start(2025-10), log(1 + rapid games) in F1, the F1 rapid share, the F1 analysed share, the F1 opportunities and the F1 opponent gap / 100.
- Reliability: for each quantity and model, on the players the model used: q on each window's odd and even observed games by game key, the change (or level) on each half, their correlation ρ over the n players with both halves, and r = 2ρ / (1 + ρ). Its 95% interval comes from Fisher's z on ρ (z ± 1.96 / √(n − 3)), mapped back and through Spearman-Brown; r_L is the lower end, floored at 0.01 (a lower end at or below 0 gives 0.01). The minimum effect per observed SD is 14 × √r_L. β / √r (at the point) and β / √r_L are reported as the disattenuated effect.
- Effect size: β_q in Elo per observed SD with its 80%, 95% and 97.5% intervals and the simultaneous interval at 1 − 0.05/9 (99.44%).
- Multiple comparisons: Holm at 0.05 across the nine quantities within each family; families are reported separately and only the primary decides.
- Reads, per quantity: "precedes climbing" when Holm rejects and β > 0; "precedes falling" when Holm rejects and β < 0; "below the minimum effect" when the simultaneous interval lies inside (−14 × √r_L, 14 × √r_L); "undetermined" otherwise (no reliability, no "below the minimum effect"); "not estimable" with fewer than 30 players.
- Attrition: staying (5 rapid games in both 2026-04 and 2026-06) on the primary's exposure and terms, a linear probability model with HC3; and the primary re-fitted by weighted least squares with weights 1 / p̂, p̂ from a logistic model of staying on the same terms, floored at 0.05. Sensitivities only.
- Diagnostics: no Bayesian model. A quantity with fewer than 30 players is not estimable; a logistic fit that fails is reported as failed.
- Exclusions and missing data: decided in section 7; a key below 5 opportunities in a window is missing for that key only; complete cases per quantity. No outlier removal.

## 9. Decision rule

Measurement on one version, read on the primary family (section 8, "below the minimum effect" against 14 × √r_L), in this order:

1. **Candidates to teach:** at least one of the eight keys reads "precedes climbing". We publish them as candidates, never as causes, and register a per-band comparison experiment of re-ordered trainability rows on new months, with this result as its development data. `trainability` does not change here; it can change only through that experiment, as a major algorithm version.
2. **No data-driven order from these keys:** no key reads "precedes climbing"; every key whose planned reliability is above the floor (W4.allowed, W5, W6, W7.prep, W7.traps and W9a, six of the eight) reads "below the minimum effect"; and the positive control (accuracy) reads "precedes climbing". `trainability` keeps its rows, and the site says plainly that the teaching order is not data-driven and that, on Lichess's analysed rapid games, a fall in these six weaknesses did not come before lasting climbing by as much as 14 Elo per SD.
3. **Need more data, for the second time:** anything else. `trainability` keeps its rows; there is no third follow-up under this rule (EXP-0009, section 9); the site keeps "no data-driven teaching order yet"; the question goes to the learning experiments (v0.6).

Outcome 2 names its six keys now, from section 6, not from the results: W4.missed and W9b cannot reach the read at any size the hash range allows, and the rule does not ask for what the design cannot give. Stage 2 is out of scope at its price (section 6). This departs from ADR 0106's measurement rule for the reason EXP-0009 gave: there is no band a link should lie in, and the control guards against reading an insensitive design as a null.

### Stopping rules

- Early stopping: none. The run is one pass once the months are streamed; a look would save little.
- Futility: the sizing step plays that part (section 6): if no hash width reaches the target, no game is observed (the kill rule of section 0).
- Budget: the run aborts at the cap of section 0; an aborted run is a failed result, reported as one. The caps outside `analysis.py` (CPU-hours, wall time, disk) stop the stage that passes them, and the decision says which.
